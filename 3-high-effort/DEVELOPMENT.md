# Velora Roads — Development Guide

Original open-world countryside racing game in the spirit of arcade-sim festival racers.
No third-party assets: every 3D model was authored through the project's Blender MCP connection,
everything else (terrain, roads, textures, sounds) is generated by project code.

## Architecture

```
scenes/main.tscn → scripts/main.gd           boot, loading screen, car spawning, GPS, autosave
autoload Game  → scripts/core/game_state.gd  roster, surfaces, assists, settings, PBs, input map, save/load
World (scripts/world/world.gd)
 ├─ WorldData      baked heights / surfaces / roads / places, spatial road queries
 ├─ SkyWeather     day/night, weather blending, fog, rain, lightning, grip + wetness globals
 ├─ Terrain        16×16 chunks of 256 m: 8 m far meshes always, 2 m near meshes + collision on worker threads
 ├─ RoadBuilder    banked road meshes, collision with surface metadata, bridges, piers, tunnel, rails, posts
 ├─ TrafficManager lane following, IDM, junction give-way, pooling, spawn ring, physics LOD, crashes
 ├─ Dressing       vegetation MultiMeshes, settlements, landmarks, signs, street lights, wind farm, festival
 │   └─ Grass      near-player grass tiles (threaded)
 └─ Activities     speed traps, zones, drift zones, jumps, races (AI), time trial, festival boards
Car (scripts/vehicle/car.gd) + CarAudio + CarFX + Autopilot (QA / AI racers)
UI (CanvasLayer): HUD+minimap, notifications, console, garage, debug menu, map, photo mode
```

Data-driven resources (`.tres`, generated by `tools/make_data.gd`):
`data/cars/*.tres` (CarSpec), `data/surfaces/*.tres` (SurfaceDef), `data/weather/*.tres` (WeatherPreset),
`data/upgrades/*.tres` (UpgradeDef), `data/events/*.tres` (EventDef). Baked world: `data/world/`
(`height.bin` 2049² float32, `surface.bin`, `roaddist.bin`, `splat.png`, `biome.png`, `world.json`).
The world plan (regions, coast, river, road control points, places) is code in `scripts/world/world_plan.gd`.

### World generation (scripts/world/world_gen.gd)
1. Road-corridor distance field from the plan (chamfer transform) softens relief near roads so roads
   follow valleys instead of fighting ridges and mesas.
2. Analytic regions: valley (rolling), highlands (radial rise toward the NW corner + ridged noise),
   mesa (plateau with flat-topped buttes), coast (cliffs in the north, beaches in the south, a bay).
3. River carved with a monotonic water level.
4. Roads: Catmull-Rom splines resampled every 4 m (the loop gets a gentle meander), height profile =
   smoothed terrain → water clearance → junction pins → iterative grade limit → span straightening
   (deep dips become straight bridges, high ground straight tunnels) → light smoothing → banking.
5. Carving: cuttings/embankments with slope-proportional falloff, lowered under banked lanes; the
   closest road segment wins where carves overlap; tunnel interiors punch terrain holes (trimesh collision there).
6. Surfaces/splat/biome textures + exact road-distance field for dressing.

Result (bake log): **40.5 km of roads in 22 roads**, Festival Loop **20.2 km** closed loop,
Valley Highway 3.8 km (4 lanes), 1 tunnel (180 m), bay bridge + river bridges, 2 hairpins.

### Vehicle physics — choice and justification
`VehicleBody3D` was evaluated from documentation and project needs: its docs state it is not designed
for realistic vehicle physics, has no gearbox/engine model, and Jolt compatibility is not documented.
The project uses its own **raycast vehicle on a Jolt `RigidBody3D`** at **120 Hz physics** (with physics
interpolation for rendering):
- one ray per wheel, spring/damper from natural frequency and damping ratio, bump stops, anti-roll bars;
- tyres: slip-angle lateral curve with peak and slide grip, longitudinal force limited by a friction circle;
  wheel spin (power oversteer) and wheel lock emerge from the circle, which is stable at 120 Hz;
- engine torque curve, clutch slip at launch, automatic gearbox with shift time and cooldown, manual mode,
  engine braking, rev limiter; drag area fitted to the stock car's power and top speed, gearing to match;
- FWD/RWD/AWD torque split, brake bias, handbrake, aero downforce front/rear;
- assists: TCS (limits drive force to the remaining grip), ABS, stability control (yaw-rate tracking
  torque), steering assist (counter-steer), automatic gears; speed-sensitive steering;
- per-surface grip by tyre type (road/sport/rally/off-road/vintage), rolling resistance, soft-surface drag,
  bumpiness; weather grip multiplier on wet-sensitive surfaces;
- air control keeps the car level after jumps; continuous collision detection enabled.

### Traffic
Lanes are derived from the road splines (right-hand traffic; the highway has two lanes per direction).
Vehicles are frozen-kinematic `RigidBody3D`s moved along the lane each physics tick, with IDM car
following (other vehicles and the player are leaders), curve speed limits with braking look-ahead,
give-way at side-road junctions, random turns at junctions, U-turns at dead ends. Spawn ring 160–650 m
outside the camera view, despawn > 820 m, pooled (33 pre-instantiated vehicles, max 34 active),
collision only within 140 m, meshes faded beyond 650 m. Density = setting × time of day. When the
player hits one it becomes a dynamic body, honks/flashes, then stops with hazards and despawns.
Types: city car, van, box lorry, coach, farm pickup, tractor (tractors only on lanes/farm tracks).

### Audio (all procedural, `scripts/audio/`)
Engine = 4 synthesized loops (low/high rpm × on/off throttle) with firing-order harmonics per cylinder
count, crank-order burble and per-cycle roughness; turbo whine and blow-off; shift clunk; tyre squeal
(paved) and gravel crunch (loose surfaces); wind; suspension thumps; impacts; horn; ambient birds,
sea, wind and rain by region and weather; thunder after lightning; reverb inside the tunnel.

### Performance (measured, RTX 5070, 1600×900 window)
See the final report for the latest numbers. Strategies: far terrain as 8 m meshes (min-filtered so
they never cover roads), near meshes + collision built on WorkerThreadPool, per-chunk vegetation
MultiMeshes with visibility ranges, grass tiles with shader distance fade, pooled traffic with physics
LOD, central managers (no per-prop `_process`), throttled spatial queries, capped shadow distance (450 m).
The map spans ±2048 m, so single-precision jitter is negligible; no origin shifting is needed.

## Blender workflow
1. Verify the MCP connection: `get_addon_status`, then `bpy.data.filepath` must equal the master path.
2. Build with the kits (cars: `carkit.build_car(spec)`; traffic: `build_car(spec, coll_path=...)`,
   `build_tractor`; props: `propkit.build_all()`), inspect with the MCP `look` tool, revise specs.
3. Export each asset to `.glb` (`carkit.export_collection` / `propkit.export_all`), save the master file.
4. `godot --headless --import`, then test in game with the QA screenshot modes.

Collections: `FORZA_GODOT/Cars/CAR_*`, `FORZA_GODOT/Traffic/TRF_*`, `FORZA_GODOT/Nature`, `Settlements`,
`Landmarks`, `Roadside`, `Festival`. Car parts are named `Body`, `Glass`, `Interior`, `SteeringWheel`,
`Wheel_FL/FR/RL/RR` (pivot at the axle), `Caliper_*`, `Lights_Head/Tail/Brake/Reverse`, plus markers
`Mk_Cockpit`, `Mk_Hood`, `Mk_Bumper` for cameras. Vehicle collision is built in code from body bounds
(two boxes) — never per-triangle.
